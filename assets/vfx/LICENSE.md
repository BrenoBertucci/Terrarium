# Third-party effect sheets

Every PNG in this directory is **CC0 1.0 (public domain dedication)**. None of
it was made for this project, and none of it is covered by whatever licence
this mod eventually ships under.

CC0 imposes no attribution requirement. The record below is kept anyway,
because a file whose provenance nobody wrote down becomes a file nobody can
safely publish.

## Source

**Free VFX asset pack** — 22 pixel-art effects authored in SpriteMancer,
released CC0 on OpenGameArt.

<https://opengameart.org/content/free-vfx-asset-pack>

Downloaded 2026-08-07 as `vfx_free_pack.zip` (94,598,540 bytes).

## What was taken, and what was changed

Eight of the pack's 22 effects were kept — the ones that read as a battle
impact in this game. The rest (worms, tentacles, vortices, constellations)
were left behind.

Only the pack's own **30fps spritesheets** were taken. The pack also ships
per-frame PNGs, GIF previews and `.smp` SpriteMancer project files; none of
those are here.

Each sheet was then **resampled down**, frame by frame, to a maximum frame
edge of 128 px. The originals ran up to 557 px per frame, which is roughly
twelve times the size these are ever drawn at in the diorama — on the target
hardware that is VRAM and bandwidth spent on detail that never reaches a
pixel. Frames were resized individually rather than the sheet as a whole, so
no frame bleeds a column of its neighbour.

Result: 1.4 MB on disk, about 15 MB of VRAM if every sheet is live at once
(they are loaded on first use, so normally far less).

| file | frame | grid | frames |
|---|---|---|---|
| `bighit.png` | 128×127 | 6×5 | 30 |
| `charged.png` | 111×128 | 7×6 | 42 |
| `electricshield.png` | 128×128 | 6×5 | 30 |
| `explosion.png` | 128×128 | 6×5 | 30 |
| `impact.png` | 124×128 | 6×5 | 30 |
| `powerchords.png` | 128×87 | 6×5 | 30 |
| `puffandstars.png` | 120×109 | 7×6 | 42 |
| `smallhit.png` | 128×127 | 6×5 | 30 |

The grid for each sheet is recorded in `data/vfx.lua`, read off the pack's own
filenames (SpriteMancer writes the frame size into the sheet name) rather than
counted by hand.

## Wind & leaf particles (cel, authored 2026-08-10)

All shapes are generated by `tools/make_wind_sprites.py` — hard-edge pixel art
(nearest filter, no soft photo-smoke). White-alpha wind sprites are tinted by
`WindFX`; leaves are full-colour flying silhouettes for `AmbientLife`.

| file | role |
| --- | --- |
| `wind_dust.png` | grit / snow speck (hard 2–3 px) |
| `wind_mote.png` | elongated seed / pollen / spray |
| `wind_puff.png` | spare cel grit cluster |
| `wind_streak.png` | tapered dash for gale / gust |
| `wind_swirl.png` | 8-frame hard-pixel curl (gust front) |
| `leaves.png` | 10× 16×16 flying leaves (green → autumn) |

Regenerate: `python tools/make_wind_sprites.py`

Earlier Kenney soft circles and kudzu *growth-stage* frames were dropped:
they read as photo dust / whole plants over a Game-Boy diorama.

## Rain impact sheets (cel, authored 2026-08-19)

Generated by `tools/make_rain_sprites.py` — hard-alpha pixel art on the same
rules as the wind particles above (nearest filter, four-step posterise, no
soft photo falloff). White RGB with the shape in alpha; `Weather` tints every
one of them by the hour's own light at draw time, so a dusk splash is warm
and a midnight one is not a white sticker.

| file | role | grid | wired |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `rain_impact.png` | crown + ejecta + ring — stone, dirt, paving | 8×2 of 64², 16 frames | no (was) |
| `rain_ripple.png` | crown + **jet** + three staggered rings — water, puddles | 8×2 of 64², 16 frames | no (was) |
| `rain_droplets.png` | spray burst, no ring — roof tile | 8×2 of 64², 16 frames | no (was) |
| `rain_mist.png` | scrolling distant-rain tile, loops vertically | 8×2 of 64², 16 frames | **no** |
| `leaf_water.png` | a leaf touching down on water (full colour) | 8×2 of 64², 16 frames | **no** |

### Status: none of these are currently drawn

They were, and they were measured, and the measurement is why they are not.

`tests/rain_cost_probe.lua` priced each half of the shower against the same
frame on Route 1, one variable at a time:

| | ms/frame |
| --- | --- |
| 440 falling drops (the streaks) | +6.4 |
| the refraction shader on those drops | −2.1 (free, inside the noise) |
| **the impact sheets** | **+21.9** |

Twenty-two milliseconds — a third of a 60 fps budget — spent on the smallest
drawings on screen, while four hundred and forty raindrops cost six. Three
`SpriteBatch` draws is excellent draw-call arithmetic and it was not what the
cost was made of.

What replaced them is geometry pushed into the mesh the rain streaks already
fill, so the whole field of impacts now costs **no draw call of its own** —
and it is per-surface in a way the three sheets could not be: water throws a
column, a puddle spreads one flat ring, stone throws a low star sideways,
grass flicks two pieces to one side, a roof tile lets the water skid downhill.
See `pushImpact` in `lib/Weather.lua`.

The PNGs are kept rather than deleted: they are authored to the spec they were
asked for, they regenerate from one script, and re-wiring them is one function.
Nothing loads them today.

The other two were never wired at all:

* `rain_mist.png` would be a worse version of a thing the mod already has.
  Distant rain is drawn in the sky shader as a continuous far curtain
  (`Sky.lua`, `curtainAmt`, led by `Weather.curtain`), and atmospheric mist
  now rides the aerial haze (`Aerial.WET_LIFT`). A tiled scrolling sprite
  over either of those would be a third distant rain on its own clock.
* `leaf_water.png` needs a seam that does not exist: nothing currently
  notices when a wind-blown leaf passes over a water cell.

Regenerate: `python tools/make_rain_sprites.py`

### Why these are authored rather than downloaded

Searched first, and the answer was not close.

The one genuinely good pixel-art water splash on OpenGameArt
(<https://opengameart.org/content/water-splash>) is **CC-BY-SA 3.0**. Share-alike
is exactly the licence this directory's rule exists to keep out: the whole
point of the CC0-only policy above is that none of it constrains what the mod
itself ships under.

The CC0 water on OGA does not do this job:

* [seamless animated raindrop ripples](https://opengameart.org/content/seamless-animated-raindrop-ripples-texture)
  — a 512² seamless tile the author describes as a heightmap, not an impact
* [Water Pack 1](https://opengameart.org/content/water-pack-1) — photographed
  river and swamp textures
* `Rain Particle Animated` — 10×10, five frames

And the record above already contains the relevant lesson: Kenney's soft
circles were pulled from this mod for reading as photo dust over a Game Boy
diorama, which is the failure mode of nearly every downloadable VFX pack in a
project that draws in hard cel steps.
